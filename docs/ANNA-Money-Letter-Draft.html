Why the ACN Is the Same

The ACN (Australian Company Number) identifies the company — JFDCO Pty Ltd. This company acts as trustee of the JFDCO Family Trust. When a company acts as trustee, it operates in a separate legal capacity. The notation "ATF" (As Trustee For) signals this distinction.

Think of it this way: a person can have a personal bank account and a business bank account. Same person, different capacity. Similarly, JFDCO Pty Ltd needs one account for its own company affairs and a separate account for the trust it manages. The ACN identifies who is operating the account; the ABN identifies in what capacity.

This is exactly why the Australian Business Register issues separate ABNs for trusts — they are independently registered entities with their own tax obligations, reporting requirements, and legal standing.

Why Separate Accounts Are Legally Required

Australian trust law requires that trust funds be kept strictly separate from the trustee company's own funds. If trust money is held in the company's account:

  • The trust may be declared invalid by a court
  • Asset protection for beneficiaries collapses
  • The ATO may treat the trust as a sham
  • The trustee director faces personal liability for breach of fiduciary duty

This is not optional — it is a fundamental legal obligation of any trustee.

ABN as a Registered Business Name

The ABN (Australian Business Number) is issued by the Australian Taxation Office through the Australian Business Register (ABR). When a trust is registered for an ABN, the full entity name — including "As Trustee For [Trust Name]" — becomes the registered business name of that entity.

This is the same mechanism by which a company can trade under different names. For example:

Example:
"ABC Pty Ltd" (ACN 123 456 789, ABN 11 123 456 789) — Company account
"ABC Pty Ltd trading as XYZ Services" (same ACN, ABN 22 987 654 321) — Trading name account

Same ACN. Different ABN. Different account. Same principle applies here.
